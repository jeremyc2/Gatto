# Website headline study

Reviewed 2026-10-23. This is copy exploration, not approved website text.

## Brief

Replace “Your image. Ready to share.” with a short, memorable thought about
showing people something on GitHub. The existing lead already explains the Mac
app, images, videos, and destinations. Keep that division of labor. Avoid feature
lists, format nouns, productivity claims, an additional eyebrow, or an additional
explanatory paragraph.

These observations describe the pages inspected today. The implications for Gatto
are editorial judgments, not claims about conversion or audience preferences.

## Collision checks

- **“Show what you mean.”** belongs to a crowded screenshot/annotation territory.
  [Creator Shots](https://www.creatorshots.com/) uses it in its hero;
  [webss.cloud](https://webss.cloud/) uses it as a section heading. Do not present
  it as a distinctive discovery.
- **“Less explaining. More showing.”** is the current
  [webss.cloud](https://webss.cloud/) hero. Reject it for Gatto.
- **“Make yourself clear.”** is the exact hero of the first-party
  [react-tourlight Studio](https://react-tourlight.vercel.app/studio), and a
  section heading in [Minty Mail](https://mintyanalyst.com/minty-mail/). It is a
  common expression that fits communication, but offers little ownable voice.
- Limited exact-phrase searches for the other candidates did not establish
  exclusivity. This is an editorial collision check, not trademark clearance.

## Original candidate exploration

Candidates below were drafted for this brief; familiar idioms are identified
where relevant. Line breaks are suggestions, not layout decisions.

| Candidate | Case for it | Reason to hesitate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Good work. / Well shown.** | Strongest editorial rhythm; warm, compact, quietly polished. Treats showing as part of doing the work. | Can suggest portfolios or polished releases. Gatto also serves ordinary questions and bug reports. The existing lead must anchor the meaning. |
| **GitHub, / illustrated.** | Strongest specificity and shortest expression of a richer GitHub conversation. The artwork makes the verbal idea tangible. | “Illustrated” can imply drawings or documentation. It does not explicitly signal motion, although the existing lead immediately supplies videos. |
| **Give GitHub / something to look at.** | Most personality. Plain language, a little irreverent, with a clear destination and no file nouns. | Eight words may be too long for the current two-line composition. Slight risk of sounding like decoration rather than communication. |
| **A little / more show.** | Light, restrained, and connected to the little-app voice. Suggests visuals without enumerating them. | An intentionally incomplete idiom; might feel coy or like a theatrical product. Weaker GitHub connection. |
| **See it. / Get it.** | Tight human payoff: understanding. Covers showing a problem, a change, or an idea. | Could suggest shopping or an image-recognition app. More punchy advertising than quiet confidence. |
| **For the record.** | Familiar idiom with a useful relationship to persistent GitHub conversations. Three words give the artwork room. | Too ambiguous by itself; can sound like recording software, legal evidence, or an archival tool. |
| **A point of view. / A place for it.** | Connects expression with somewhere to share it; the parallel rhythm suits an editorial page. | Nine words, abstract, and can imply an opinion platform. Cleverness outruns clarity. |
| **Show them / the point.** | Human audience, action, and annotation in one thought. | Sounds adversarial; puts annotations ahead of the broader sharing purpose. |
| **Make yourself clear.** | Strong direct human benefit and four familiar words. | Exact product-copy collisions above; writing-tool ambiguity. Retain only as a benchmark for economy. |
| **Point taken.** | A familiar human response with a second reading around annotations. Two words; excellent restraint. | Passive, can sound defeated in an argument, and puts annotation at the center. The GitHub relationship relies entirely on the lead. |
| **GitHub gets / the picture.** | Warmer platform-specific option. The idiom ties showing to understanding, with a little personality. | Personifies the platform when people do the understanding. Slightly more advertising-like and less editorial than “GitHub, illustrated.” |

## Judgment

The strongest recommendation is **GitHub, illustrated.**: the platform gives it
specificity and the second word supplies the visual thought. The strongest
alternatives are **Point taken.** for extreme restraint and **GitHub gets the
picture.** for warmth. **Good work. Well shown.** is the best additional
editorial direction, but less closely tied to GitHub conversation. These serve
different tastes; none needs an
extra sentence to defend it. The current lead should remain the explanatory
anchor. Avoid presenting a large list of near-identical options to the user.
