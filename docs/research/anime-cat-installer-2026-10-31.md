# Anime cat anatomy for the installer

Researched October 31, 2026 after the installer revision produced pinched
ankles and separate boot-shaped feet. The purpose is an original anime cat
rendered as pixel art; reference images inform construction, not copied pixels
or a replacement character design.

## Inspected anime references

- [Studio Ghibli, The Cat Returns gallery](https://www.ghibli.jp/works/baron/),
  especially [still 002](https://www.ghibli.jp/gallery/baron002.jpg). Inspected
  enlarged in the official gallery: the cat tumbles with all paws raised. The
  haunch flows into the limb without a separate circular joint, and the raised
  paws finish continuous curved volumes. There is no narrow neck immediately
  before a separate oval foot. This is a volume/simplification reference, not
  the installer pose.
- [The Cat Returns, still 003](https://www.ghibli.jp/gallery/baron003.jpg).
  Inspected enlarged: folded forelegs use simple uninterrupted contours and
  small rounded ends. Its anthropomorphic standing pose should not determine
  the installer's four-legged flight anatomy.
- [Suzume official character page](https://suzume-tojimari-movie.jp/index.html),
  [Daijin still](https://suzume-tojimari-movie.jp/assets/images/ver3/character/image3.jpg).
  Inspected at native 680 × 390: the kitten hangs head-down with relaxed,
  extended forelegs. A long gentle taper leads into a blunt, rounded paw; there
  is no pinched wrist, separate foot blob or visible individual digit line in
  this pose. One long cel-shadow plane follows the limb. Shoulder roots are
  naturally obscured by the large head. This is particularly useful for the
  installer's reaching forelegs, despite the different coat and orientation.
- [Studio Ghibli, Kiki's Delivery Service gallery](https://www.ghibli.jp/works/majo/)
  was also reviewed. It supplies the black anime-cat silhouette context, but the
  available stills offered limited clear side-on leaping-leg information. Do
  not claim this gallery alone establishes the installer's joint placement.

These observations describe the inspected drawings, not a universal rule for
all anime cats. No single source above supplies the exact installer pose.

## Structural cross-check

The [University of Illinois veterinary imaging atlas](https://vetmed.illinois.edu/imaging_anatomy/)
separates feline forelimb radius/ulna from carpus/foot and hindlimb femur from
tibia/fibula and tarsus/foot. Its
[feline tarsus/foot example](https://vetmed.illinois.edu/imaging_anatomy/feline/hindlimb/tarsus_foot/ex01/tar-foot01-f%20.html)
is an anatomical cross-check, not an appearance template. A stylized hock bend
should still belong to one continuous limb, rather than an extra ankle segment.

[Burkholder and Nichols, A Three Dimensional Model of the Feline Hindlimb](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2043500/)
models hip, knee and ankle separately and defines thigh, shank and foot by bony
landmarks. This supports constructing a gesture through successive segments
before drawing the outside contour. Its laboratory stance angles are not a
reference for a flying pose. The model does not justify exaggerating visible
joints or pinching the silhouette around every anatomical landmark.

## Translation into this pixel-art scene

The following are drawing decisions inferred from the references, not quoted
anatomical requirements:

1. Establish one broad gesture and continuous filled silhouette per leg before
   shading. The near haunch should merge into the rump. Indicate a bend with a
   shallow change of direction and an overlapping muscle plane, not a deep notch.
2. Keep enough width through the lower leg to carry the fur volume. Avoid a
   skinny connector between the leg and a wide horizontal foot. Round off the
   same volume into a compact paw, with a modest width change.
3. Let the near limb obscure part of the far limb. A small clear negative-space
   gap and quieter shading can separate the pairs; outlining every hidden
   segment creates an articulated toy appearance.
4. On reaching forelegs, use the Daijin reference's supple taper and compact end.
   Avoid mitten-like circles, long finger grooves and an outline around the
   wrist. A short toe crease is optional if it helps at final display size.
5. Keep the existing black-purple coat and approved face. Purple models dark
   fur volume; it should not turn the limbs into bright lavender sausages.

[Saint11's Cluster Sketching and Painting](https://saint11.art/pixel_art_articles/article2/)
starts with large color masses and gesture before detail, then refines cluster
boundaries and staircase logic. Apply that order here: judge the leg silhouette
in one color, shade with a few long connected planes, and add only essential paw
accents. Near a curve, increase or decrease pixel run lengths gradually; do not
add alternating tiny steps to simulate anatomy. The same artist's
[Consistency article](https://saint11.art/blog/consistency/)
supports reusing established colors and integer nearest-neighbor scaling.

## Review gates

- At actual installer size, all four limbs read as feline volumes, including
  the partly occluded far limbs; neither hind paw reads as a boot below a narrow
  vertical ankle.
- In a silhouette-only close-up, each lower leg transitions smoothly into its paw.
- Check the full scene before magnified toe details. Pixel lint can find detached
  pixels; it cannot certify believable anatomy.
- Keep the accepted bushy tail and broad background coverage. Exactly two
  exhaust roots correspond to two tightly overlapping barrels. For flame shape,
  colors and internal proportions, the repository's approved portal-cat flame
  layer is the controlling visual reference; anime-cat research does not define
  the exhaust design.
