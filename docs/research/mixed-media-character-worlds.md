# Cats across materials: a small, coherent multiverse

Research date: October 16, 2026 (America/Chicago). This note supports the [mixed-world study](../design/explorations/multiverse-study.html). It records production precedents and proposes experiments; it does not approve a cast, website layout, or final visual system.

## Starting with the preferences we actually have

The user's first choice is the detailed watercolor black kitten with cyan eyes; second is the kitten built from overlapping dark paper. Several luminous blue-violet treatments remain useful alternatives. The additional luminous sheet identified as unnecessary should leave the active shortlist. These rankings come from this conversation, not a general audience study.

Keep the black cat as the primary identity. There are no dogs in the proposed story. A secondary cat might have a different color or material, or we might see the same cat across worlds. That distinction remains open. The app's existing character treatment should remain a candidate alongside the new art. The immediate question is whether these characters can share an intelligible moment while preserving the particular qualities the user loves.

## What the production sources establish

**Different worlds can have different visual rules.** In a first-person interview, _Across the Spider-Verse_ FX artist Nikolaos Finizio explains that an effect must follow the style of its world: an explosion in Gwen's environment differs from one in Spider-Man 2099's. Supervisor Pav Grochola describes art-department references guiding the desired look. Finizio also describes passes moving between 2D and 3D artists, with the final approach chosen for the shot. This is evidence of coordinated, scene-specific art direction rather than a universal finishing filter. [Finizio and Grochola interview, Toon Boom](https://www.toonboom.com/behind-the-amazing-2dfx-in-spider-man-across-the-spider-verse)

**Style can express a feeling and still depend on staging.** Sony describes Gwen's watercolor backgrounds moving with her moods. The same production feature explains how the team explored her apartment to choose blocking and shots with emotional impact. Its Vulture example retains the quality of a living drawing. The source supports both expressive materials and deliberate staging; it does not prescribe a particular recipe for combining our cats. [Sony, _Beyond the Screen_](https://www.sony.com/en/brand/beyondthescreen/acrossthespiderverse/)

**Materials need to respond to the scene.** In Foundry's first-person production account, Imageworks head of compositing Marco Recuay describes balancing texture, lighting, effects, and composition. He explains that Scarlet Spider's hatching needed to react to illumination, movement, and deformation. For this research, the useful observation is that a drawn surface participates in the character's physical presentation. We do not need the film's hatching, printing effects, or production machinery. [Recuay production account, Foundry](https://colorway.foundry.com/insights/film-tv/across-the-spider-verse-nuke-mari-katana)

**A story object can connect worlds.** DC's original _Multiversity_ announcement explains a device in which each world publishes comics about heroes living in the others; realizing that relationship connects the characters. This is evidence for the announced narrative mechanism, not a claim about every detail of the final series. Our transferable idea is a recognizable object that crosses a boundary and gives the characters a reason to meet. [DC, _Multiversity Is Coming!_](https://www.dc.com/blog/2012/09/29/multiversity-is-coming)

**Mixed media does not require multiverse lore.** Production studio Soi describes _The Amazing World of Gumball_ combining drawn animation, puppetry, CGI, stop motion, and live action in a shared everyday setting. Its account establishes coexistence, not a controlled test of why viewers accept it. It gives us permission to explore different materials without explaining portals, timelines, or cosmology. [Studio Soi production page](https://www.studiosoi.de/gumball)

**Expressive performance can support stylistic variety.** The original _Into the Spider-Verse_ production account explains that emotional character performance supported the film’s highly stylized look. Its published gallery and Andy Bialk’s first-party development portfolio show deliberate character and proportion exploration. Our use is limited to the process lesson: individual shapes and gestures should carry character while a shared encounter makes their relationship readable. We are not importing the films’ printing effects or superhero vocabulary. [Imageworks production account and gallery](https://www.imageworks.com/node/1371), [Bialk’s development portfolio](https://www.andybialk.com/spiderman-into-the-spiderverse)

## Our proposed rules to test

Everything below is a Gatto design hypothesis inferred from the precedents and the supplied artwork. These are not Sony's documented rules, and their success still needs visual judgment.

**Give each encounter one place.** Begin with a quiet surface or threshold shared by two cats. Agree on the ground, approximate scale, camera angle, and where their paws meet an object. Eyelines should converge on the interaction. Use a small contact shadow or overlap consistent with each material. We should be able to cover the textures and still understand who is doing what.

**Preserve the materials.** Watercolor can retain pigment variation, fibrous paper, and soft lost edges. The paper kitten can retain layered fur, cut contours, and small construction shadows. The app-like cat can retain its clearer shapes and luminous accents. Cohesion should emerge from the shared action and composition. Turning every cat into the same airbrushed finish would erase the reason for this experiment.

**Choose a host for each scene.** In one study the encounter happens on watercolor paper, with the paper kitten as a visitor. In another it happens on a model-maker's table. The host supplies most of the background and atmosphere; a guest keeps its own surface treatment. Start with two materials. A third needs a story job before it needs a place in the frame.

**Keep identity separate from medium.** Compare the same ear shape, cyan eye family, dark coat, generous head, curled tail, and attentive posture across treatments. Those are candidate anchors, not a finalized character sheet. In the active postcards, avoid repeated poses: distinguish cats through their action, proportions, eyes, and material, while preserving clear identity anchors. A new color alone may suggest a different individual; identical markings alone may not communicate the same individual.

**Let one image travel.** A small picture card could move between cats. Keep its recognizable subject and orientation while the surrounding rendering changes. This makes the encounter purposeful and loosely alludes to helping an image reach its destination. It must remain an illustration metaphor: it should not suggest that Gatto repaints images, changes their contents, or offers an art-generation feature.

## Three studies before any commitment

1. **The handoff:** one watercolor kitten and one paper kitten attend to the same picture card. Ask whether they feel present together and whether the recipient looks like a companion or another rendering of the sender.
2. **The shared discovery:** a watercolor cat and an app-like visitor inspect one image from different positions, with distinct actions and proportions. Ask what connects them without making their materials identical.
3. **The quiet visitor:** an app-like black cat notices a paper or watercolor cat across a simple material boundary. Ask whether the boundary helps explain the difference or becomes unnecessary scenery.

Judge the stills first. Ask which cat attracts attention first, whether both touch the same world, what action is occurring, and what feels pasted in. Then ask which material differences are worth protecting. Motion is a later question; it should not conceal an unresolved still composition. No comic-panel framing, superhero poses, halftone effects, or Spider-Verse color formula is required to test these ideas.
