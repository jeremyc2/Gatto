# Popular 2D indie games: what feels good, and what invites another session

Research date: October 18, 2026 (America/Chicago). Focus: player enjoyment and reasons to return, without implementation details.

## The central finding

The strongest shared idea is **a satisfying action now, paired with a personally meaningful possibility next**. That possibility might be “I can beat this room,” “this combination could work,” “I want to see what is beyond that door,” or “I want to spend another evening with these people.” Those are different pleasures. A game does not need every one of them.

This is a design interpretation of the cases below, not a measured explanation of their commercial success. The sources establish the games' features, developers' intentions, and audience milestones. They do not isolate what caused sales or provide comparable long-term retention figures.

## Scope and how to read the evidence

The main cohort emphasizes games released between **October 18, 2016 and October 18, 2026**, counting early access. “Of the last decade” can also mean games people kept playing during that decade, so a separate section includes older, especially instructive examples. Stardew Valley, for example, arrived just before this release window.

“Popular” means a substantial demonstrated audience, rather than a definitive worldwide ranking. This is a curated selection spanning different kinds of fun. Sales, monthly active users, and Steam reviews measure different things and must not be ranked against one another. Historical milestones below are lower-bound evidence of reach, not estimates of present sales or active players. The dated Steam snapshots count **English-language reviews shown by the store**, not owners, all-language reviews, or repeat players.

“2D” here includes side-on games and games played across a top-down plane. Hades and Cult of the Lamb are useful comparators with dimensional-looking presentation; a definition requiring exclusively flat artwork would narrow the selection. Independent development can coexist with an external publisher. The study concerns player experience, rather than a test of every company's financing or ownership.

Release dates also need care: a demo or separate precursor is not the commercial game's launch. The 2018 Celeste is distinct from the earlier Celeste Classic linked on its [official site](https://www.celestegame.com/). Hades first reached early access on Epic in December 2018, a year before Steam; its [developer FAQ](https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/hades-faq/) makes that distinction explicit. Vampire Survivors' [developer-published itch page](https://poncle.itch.io/vampire-survivors) dates its public build to March 31, 2021; its [Steam listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1794680/Vampire_Survivors/) distinguishes early access in December 2021 from 1.0 in October 2022.

## What makes each game work

Each case separates **documented ingredients** from **interpretation**. The imagined player thoughts illustrate motivations; they are not quotations from surveyed players. “Tension” describes a design tradeoff to consider, not a measured complaint rate.

### Hollow Knight and Silksong: curiosity becomes earned familiarity

**Documented ingredients.** Hollow Knight offers an interconnected world, route choice, new movement abilities, equippable charms, mapping tools, secrets, and an unlockable Steel Soul mode. Its free expansions add quests and bosses. Silksong pairs exploration with Hornet's acrobatic combat, tools, quests, and an additional challenge after completion. [Hollow Knight store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/367520/Hollow_Knight/), [Silksong store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1030300/Hollow_Knight_Silksong/)

**Interpretation — pleasure now.** A forbidding place gradually becomes somewhere you understand. Movement and fighting make the journey physically satisfying; unexpected characters and landscapes give that competence emotional texture.

**Interpretation — next session.** “There was a path I couldn't reach.” An unfinished map can be more compelling than a checklist because the player supplies the question. Later returns can pursue mastery, alternate routes, or optional challenges. Much of the first journey's appeal is finite discovery, rather than endless replay.

**Tension.** Getting lost supports wonder but can become aimlessness. Hard fights make conquest meaningful but can interrupt the desire to explore. Silksong's large launch audience demonstrates demand; it does not establish years of enduring replay.

### Cuphead: the thrill of finally performing the impossible

**Documented ingredients.** Cuphead concentrates on boss battles, with weapons, super moves, secrets, solo play, and local co-op. Its cartoon characters, watercolor scenery, and jazz are central to its presentation. The later Delicious Last Course expansion introduces another island and Ms. Chalice. [Official store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/268910/Cuphead/), [Studio MDHR history](https://studiomdhr.com/about-us/)

**Interpretation — pleasure now.** An enemy is both an obstacle and a performance worth watching. A successful dodge feels like joining the choreography. The pleasure comes from changing “this is chaos” into “I know what to do.”

**Interpretation — next session.** A nearly successful attempt leaves a specific unfinished goal: beat that boss, try another weapon, or succeed together with a friend. Replaying familiar encounters can turn survival into a cleaner performance. An expansion supplies genuinely new encounters, a different reason to return.

**Tension.** Repetition can build confidence or produce exhaustion. Artwork may attract someone who does not enjoy sustained challenge. Its success does not mean every campaign needs endless play after the ending.

### Celeste: difficult work that feels like encouragement

**Documented ingredients.** Celeste combines jumping, dashing, and climbing with fast respawns, secrets, harder B-side chapters, and Madeline's story of self-discovery. Designer Maddy Thorson explains that many small allowances favor the player's intended action, helping a difficult game feel kind. [Official store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/504230/Celeste/), [Thorson, “Celeste & Forgiveness”](https://www.mattmakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/index.html)

**Interpretation — pleasure now.** A daunting screen becomes a sequence you can execute. Immediate retries keep attention on the adjustment you want to make. The story can make persistence feel compassionate rather than humiliating.

**Interpretation — next session.** “I understand the room; I can finish it.” Optional challenges extend the same expressive movement into a higher ceiling. The main story can also be a complete, satisfying experience: returning is an opportunity, not a prerequisite for having enjoyed it.

**Tension.** A quick restart reduces waiting, but does not make repeated failure universally enjoyable. Optional difficulty works best when the player experiences it as an invitation.

### Dead Cells: momentum, danger, and a fresh way to fight

**Documented ingredients.** Dead Cells combines responsive combat with changing environments, branching routes, distinct weapons, secrets, and a run-ending death. Some unlocks persist into later attempts. Motion Twin describes the original game as complete after 35 major updates and five DLCs. [Official game site](https://deadcells.com/), [store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/588650/Dead_Cells/), [Motion Twin FAQ](https://motiontwin.com/faq)

**Interpretation — pleasure now.** Speed and improvisation make individual fights pleasurable before any distant reward arrives. Finding unfamiliar equipment asks, “What sort of fighter am I this time?” Danger gives a good run stakes.

**Interpretation — next session.** A loss can leave both a lesson and another possibility: a route to explore, an unlock to use, a fighting style to try. The game mixes improving the player with expanding the player's options.

**Tension.** Losing a long attempt can outweigh the small gains carried forward. Too many additions can obscure a clear core. Its completed content history is useful evidence that continued enjoyment need not depend on an unending update schedule; the sources do not measure post-update retention.

### Hades: a failed escape can still move your life forward

**Documented ingredients.** Hades varies weapons and divine boons between escape attempts, preserves upgrades, and develops relationships and story events across returns home. It offers God Mode and optional difficulty modifiers. Supergiant explicitly describes characters who remember the player across attempts. [Official store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1145360/Hades/), [developer FAQ](https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/hades-faq/)

**Interpretation — pleasure now.** Fast fighting supplies immediate energy; choosing powers gives the attempt an identity. The cast makes the place you return to worth inhabiting.

**Interpretation — next session.** “That build almost worked” and “I want to hear what they say next” reinforce each other. A failed escape can still yield a relationship scene, knowledge, or progress, so failure does not erase every kind of accomplishment. Once the story is familiar, build experimentation and chosen difficulty can continue independently.

**Tension.** Permanent upgrades can blur whether success came from learning or accumulation. Dialogue eventually becomes familiar. The design lesson is to offer several meaningful forms of progress, while keeping the central action satisfying in its own right.

### Slay the Spire: the pleasure of becoming a better judge

**Documented ingredients.** Each attempt offers different cards, relics, encounters, and route risks. The game includes daily and custom modes. Cofounder Anthony Giovannetti describes the goal of giving every card a useful place while avoiding choices that dominate everything, alongside 20 successive Ascension difficulty levels. [Official store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/646570/Slay_the_Spire/), [developer's GDC 2019 presentation](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2019/presentations/Giovannetti_Anthony_SlayTheSpire.pdf)

**Interpretation — pleasure now.** You make a small judgment, see its consequences, and reconsider what the deck needs. The thrill is not merely finding a strong card; it is recognizing when an unlikely choice becomes right.

**Interpretation — next session.** “I would handle that decision differently.” New circumstances make accumulated understanding useful without making every decision automatic. Difficulty goals give that learning direction.

**Tension.** Bad luck can feel indistinguishable from bad judgment. If one strategy always wins, discovery collapses into repetition.

**2026 continuation.** Slay the Spire 2 sold 3 million units in its first early-access week and recorded more than 25 million attempted runs, with incomplete initial tracking, by March 13, 2026. Those are purchases and aggregate attempts, not a retention rate. [Mega Crit](https://www.megacrit.com/news/2026-3-13-neowsletter-issue-20/)

### Vampire Survivors: the transformation from prey into spectacle

**Documented ingredients.** Vampire Survivors presents survival against growing hordes, choices that can produce rapid power growth, and gold collected for the next survivor. The full game offers freely refundable power-ups, co-op, and additional content packs. [Developer-published itch page](https://poncle.itch.io/vampire-survivors), [official store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1794680/Vampire_Survivors/)

**Interpretation — pleasure now.** The central emotional arc is a reversal: the swarm that frightened you becomes something your growing power overwhelms. Collecting rewards and seeing attacks expand make progress visible while positioning still matters.

**Interpretation — next session.** “What happens if I lean into a different set of powers?” A familiar survival premise supports a new transformation. Persistent upgrades add another reason to continue; content packs add fresh material to investigate.

**Tension.** Spectacle can become visual noise, and repeated collection can become routine. “More things on screen” is useful only while choices still change the experience. The transferable idea is a palpable change in what the player can handle.

### Balatro: I made these ordinary cards do something outrageous

**Documented ingredients.** Balatro combines poker hands with Jokers and other modifiers, escalating score requirements, distinct decks, eight difficulty settings, challenges, and seeded runs. The collection reveals additional cards and secrets through play. [Official store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2379780/Balatro/)

**Developer's account.** LocalThunk says the familiar cards help introduce the game, and describes the deeper strategy as reducing risk while assembling enough power to win. He also explains that an overly powerful option can crowd out other strategies. These are his own answers in a direct interview, rather than the publication's sales commentary. [LocalThunk interview, March 7, 2024](https://rogueliker.com/balatro-interview/)

**Interpretation — pleasure now.** Familiar hands make the opening understandable. Unexpected interactions then create a much larger result than the cards initially suggested. Watching a plan pay off feels partly like discovery and partly like authorship.

**Interpretation — next session.** “That interaction suggests another deck.” A successful combination is satisfying now and an advertisement for unexplored combinations. Increasing demands keep the player balancing immediate survival against a plan that might become powerful later.

**Tension.** A favorite plan may not receive the pieces it needs. Unlocks and escalating numbers alone cannot sustain interest if the underlying choices become rote. The useful lesson is to let combinations alter the player's plan, rather than merely inflate a score.

### Cult of the Lamb: adventure matters because someone is waiting at home

**Documented ingredients.** The game joins randomized expeditions and combat to building a settlement, tending followers, performing rituals, and expanding the cult. Subsequent content adds follower traits, postgame challenges, and local co-op. [Official store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1313140/Cult_of_the_Lamb/)

**Interpretation — pleasure now.** Combat and caretaking provide contrasting moods. Cute followers and dark rituals create a distinctive comic relationship with responsibility: this is your strange little community.

**Interpretation — next session.** “I'll improve the settlement, then make one more expedition.” One activity gives the other purpose. Relationships and visible changes offer a more personal continuity than a run result alone; co-op can make that place shared.

**Tension.** Needy followers can turn attachment into an obligation, while settlement chores can interrupt the activity the player currently prefers. The two halves need to make each other desirable. Their mere coexistence does not guarantee that either is fun.

### Brotato: a compact experiment with an obvious next hypothesis

**Documented ingredients.** Brotato offers up to six weapons, distinct characters, many items, automatic firing by default, short enemy waves, and shopping between waves. Its developer advertises runs under 30 minutes and adjustable enemy difficulty. [Official store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1942280/Brotato/)

**Interpretation — pleasure now.** Alternate between dodging under pressure and calmly deciding what to buy. Each shopping break makes a change you can soon feel. Character restrictions make a new attempt begin with a recognizable premise.

**Interpretation — next session.** “Can I make this character work?” The next experiment is easy to name and relatively small to begin. Unlike an open-ended adventure, the game offers a compact test of a build idea.

**Tension.** A short format can still become repetitive when opening waves feel predetermined. A large catalog of characters matters only if it changes decisions. The appeal is the clear experiment and its consequences, not the item count by itself.

### Among Us: other people generate the surprise

**Documented ingredients.** Crewmates complete tasks, gather information, discuss deaths, and vote on suspected impostors. Impostors deceive, sabotage, and kill. Roles are assigned among a group of 4–15; the developer recommends playing with friends and voice chat. [Official store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/945360/Among_Us/), [Innersloth press kit](https://www.innersloth.com/press-kit-among-us/)

**Interpretation — pleasure now.** The game gives ordinary conversation stakes. Noticing a contradiction, successfully lying, or realizing a trusted friend fooled you produces a story the group can retell.

**Interpretation — next session.** “This time I'll catch you.” The map can be familiar while intentions and alliances change. Returning is partly a choice to spend time with the group; novelty does not require another authored level.

**Tension.** Enjoyment depends heavily on the people, their willingness to play along, and how much information they share. Elimination can leave someone with less interesting participation. Its enormous 2020 audience establishes reach, not uninterrupted popularity or a current retention rate.

## The near-boundary case: Stardew Valley

Stardew Valley released on February 26, 2016, almost eight months before the strict rolling cutoff. Excluding it from the release cohort is precise; excluding its lessons from a study of the decade's enduring 2D indie appeal would be unhelpful. ConcernedApe reported **50 million copies sold on February 26, 2026**. He also described new and returning players arriving daily, which is a first-party qualitative observation, not a published retention dataset. [Official about page](https://www.stardewvalley.net/about/), [tenth-anniversary post](https://www.stardewvalley.net/stardew-valley-10-year-anniversary/)

**Documented ingredients.** Farming, fishing, mining, relationships, seasonal festivals, skill growth, and decorating let the player develop a home and a place in a community. [Official description](https://www.stardewvalley.net/about/)

**Interpretation.** Care becomes visible: an overgrown field becomes *my* farm. The next session has overlapping promises—harvest something, improve a tool, get to know someone, prepare for a season. The player can switch activities without abandoning the larger life they are building. Familiarity becomes comforting rather than merely exhausted content. A new farm or shared play can provide a different set of priorities.

**Tension.** The same routines that provide structure can feel like chores; trying to maximize every day can crowd out relaxation. Its distinctive lesson is that returning can mean belonging somewhere and tending something, rather than overcoming a harder opponent. Updates can also renew curiosity: the anniversary post explicitly frames a forthcoming update as a reason for lapsed players to return, without establishing a release date or a measured effect. [ConcernedApe](https://www.stardewvalley.net/stardew-valley-10-year-anniversary/)

## Different kinds of coming back

This comparison is an interpretive map of the documented cases, not a player segmentation study.

| Reason to return | What the next session promises | Representative cases | What can undermine it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Finish a meaningful journey | Another place, revelation, or ending | Hollow Knight, Silksong, Celeste | Losing direction; assuming everyone wants endless play |
| Master a familiar challenge | A cleaner performance or a once-impossible success | Cuphead, Celeste, Dead Cells | Failure that teaches little or wastes too much time |
| Test an idea | A new combination, decision, or constraint | Slay the Spire, Balatro, Brotato, Vampire Survivors | One dominant answer; changes that are only numerical |
| Develop a relationship or home | Someone remembers me; something I care for grows | Hades, Cult of the Lamb, Stardew Valley | Progress that starts feeling compulsory |
| Make something or continue a personal story | A building project, a growing colony, an unexpected turn | Terraria, RimWorld | Too little direction; consequences that feel arbitrary |
| Rejoin a group | New stories with familiar people | Among Us; co-op in several other cases | A poor social experience or difficulty assembling the group |
| Explore something newly added | Fresh situations in an already loved game | Expansions, substantial updates, sequels | Treating fresh content as a substitute for enjoyable existing play |

The first six can arise from the game already in someone's hands. The last depends on additional material. They can reinforce each other, but they are not the same phenomenon. A returning player exploring a new expansion is different evidence from someone replaying an unchanged challenge.

The sources also do not separate new buyers from returning players in review activity or cumulative sales. A sequel's successful launch shows continuing interest in a series; it does not show that the original game retained all those players. Among Us' 2020 monthly audience, Silksong's launch-year purchases, and Stardew's cumulative sales answer different questions.

## Practical lessons to carry forward

These are design hypotheses derived from the comparisons, not a formula validated against unsuccessful games.

1. **Choose the feeling before the reward system.** Becoming graceful, feeling clever, discovering a place, caring for a home, and fooling friends suggest different activities. A generic progress bar cannot choose that identity for a game.
2. **Make the ordinary minute enjoyable.** The central action should be worth doing before the next unlock arrives. Ask what feels good in a minute where no new content is revealed.
3. **Leave a question the player can own.** “Could this combination work?” or “What is over there?” gives a session personal purpose. It is stronger than an obligation to clear another notification.
4. **Make a setback suggest an adjustment.** A player can accept losing when they can name what to try next. Reduce the time between learning something and testing it, while preserving enough consequence for success to matter.
5. **Change decisions, not just quantities.** A new option earns its place when it changes priorities, movement, risk, or relationships. A larger catalog that produces the same decisions is a weaker source of variety.
6. **Let progress live in more than one place.** Knowledge, confidence, an altered home, a relationship, and an unlocked option can each carry meaning between sessions. They need not all be currencies.
7. **Give play a rhythm and a stopping place.** Pressure followed by reflection, or adventure followed by home, can keep one activity from exhausting its welcome. A satisfying stopping point also makes the next beginning understandable.
8. **Treat a memorable ending as success.** Discovery and story have natural limits. Optional mastery and replay can serve the people who want more without making the initial experience feel incomplete.

If choosing a few games to study closely, the most useful contrasts are **Celeste** for encouraging mastery, **Slay the Spire or Balatro** for decisions that stay interesting, **Hades** for meaningful failure, **Hollow Knight** for curiosity, **Stardew Valley** for attachment to a place, and **Among Us** for human unpredictability. That is a choice of contrasting lessons, not another popularity ranking.
