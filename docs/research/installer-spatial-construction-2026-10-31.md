# Installer: limb and exhaust construction

Researched October 31, 2026 for the complete redraw requested in the user's
mood board. These references inform an original drawing; their pixels are not
an input to the exported asset. The earlier
[anime-cat study](anime-cat-installer-2026-10-31.md) remains useful for expression,
but its advice about continuous volumes was insufficient: a smooth silhouette
can still conceal an implausible skeleton.

## Limbs and paws

The [Illinois veterinary imaging atlas](https://vetmed.illinois.edu/imaging_anatomy/)
separates shoulder, elbow, forearm, carpus and foot, and the hindlimb's femur,
stifle, tibia and tarsus. Its
[feline tarsus radiographs](https://vetmed.illinois.edu/imaging_anatomy/feline/hindlimb/tarsus_foot/ex01/tar-foot01-f%20.html)
were inspected visually: the heel projects behind the ankle joint and the long
metatarsals continue toward the toes. The apparent backward bend is not the
knee. The example has soft-tissue swelling, so it is a bone-layout cross-check,
not a normal coat contour template. The
[feline elbow example](https://vetmed.illinois.edu/imaging_anatomy/feline/forelimb/elbow/ex01/elbo01-f%20.html)
provides lateral and front views of the separate upper-arm/forearm joint.

[Wakuri's primary study of domestic-cat foot pads](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmammsocjapan1952/2/2/2_2_35/_pdf)
examined 18 adult cats. It distinguishes digital pads, a large metacarpal or
metatarsal pad, and the forelimb's carpal pad. Its text describes the large pad
as roughly heart-shaped, pointing toward the toes, with variable lateral
lobes. Digital pads are smaller, oval or triangular. Figure 3 records forepaw
variations, and figure 4 records hindpaw variations. The first forepaw digit is
smallest and is absent in the hindpaw. The study establishes the organization;
it does not imply every pad is biologically identical or perfectly symmetric.
The PDF text was read; its diagrams did not render in the research browser.

Two official Studio Ghibli stills were inspected visually:

- [The Cat Returns, still 021](https://www.ghibli.jp/gallery/baron021.jpg): a cat
  reaches both forelegs toward the viewer. Shoulder roots blend into the chest;
  the blunt toe silhouette turns around a broad central sole pad. One coherent
  shadow plane follows each limb. It supports foreshortening and readable soles,
  not the installer's exact pose or a full four-pad anatomy template.
- [Still 002](https://www.ghibli.jp/gallery/baron002.jpg): a tumbling cat raises its
  feet. Direction changes happen through a whole limb rather than through
  independent balls joined by narrow connectors. It is a simplified anime
  drawing; its anthropomorphic torso should not determine kitten proportions.

Drawing decisions inferred from these references:

1. Mark the skeleton before the outline. On a hind leg, place hip, stifle,
   hock and toe base separately. Flex the resulting zigzag for flight, with the
   upper thigh partly hidden in the rump. Do not let an oval thigh taper directly
   into a foot, which reads as a drumstick.
2. Give the foreleg a shoulder, an elbow behind/below it, and a forearm reaching
   forward. Avoid a separate round shoulder lump, a pinched wrist, or a broad
   foot attached to a thin connector. Suggest a joint with a change in contour
   direction and an overlapping fur plane; do not outline every bone.
3. Establish each sole's centerline and toe edge. Place four ordinary toe pads
   in a shallow arch across that edge and one broad main pad behind them. A
   dewclaw or carpal pad is separate and may be hidden. Do not arrange five
   equal bubbles around the perimeter.
4. Draw both forepaws from the same anatomical plan, then foreshorten the far
   one. Consistent pad count, orientation and spacing should remain visually
   obvious at installer size. Mathematical transformation alone does not
   guarantee that they read as a matching pair.
5. Use overlap and lower contrast to put far limbs behind the body. A small
   negative-space gap can establish the pair without exposing every segment.

## Two exhaust roots, one large downstream volume

[NASA's X-2 twin-chamber flight photograph](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/x-2-twin-set-of-shock-diamonds/)
and its [full-size image](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/341151main_E-2822_full.jpg)
were inspected visually. Two narrow roots remain parallel, with two axial
sequences of bright cells. Their surrounding exhaust becomes less distinct
farther downstream. NASA identifies the cells as shock diamonds in supersonic
exhaust. This is a geometry reference, not a requirement to paint literal
diamonds or copy the photographed engine arrangement.

[NASA's HiDyRS-X test account, August 2016, pages 5–6](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/augustlagniappe_0.pdf)
explains that ordinary exposure can hide plume structure in excessive
brightness. Multiple exposures revealed moving plume vortices and nozzle
motion. This supports preserving visible internal flow in the artwork; it
does not prescribe rainbow bands or one particular plume color.

Drawing decisions:

1. Fix the barrel axis and the center of each rear mouth first. Both exhaust
   centerlines are backward continuations of those axes. A luminous stripe on a
   barrel's front cap must not be mistaken for the exhaust mouth.
2. Keep two short, hot core tracks visible near their exits, with the far one
   partly occluded. Blend their outer contours downstream. Separation should
   come from depth and a small value difference, not divergent angles.
3. Build the exaggerated outer plume from overlapping flowing masses and
   connected wisps, with a shared overall direction. Avoid a smooth closed
   leaf, pointed oval, concentric rainbow shell, or two forked tongues.
4. Use the approved cat's white/yellow/orange heat, violet transition, and
   blue/cyan edges as artistic color references. Color is not a realism test;
   origin, direction, expansion and overlap establish spatial plausibility.
5. Review the exhaust alone, then with the tail and body. Both mouth connections
   must survive compositing. The tail should occlude flame naturally rather
   than forcing the flame to curl around it.

These are construction checks, not proof of a successful illustration. Judge
the silhouette, emotional expression and depth in the full installer preview.
