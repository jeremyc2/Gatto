## Interpretation to carry forward

- **Coconat is the leading display-font candidate.** The Shared discovery postcard uses Coconat. The stated reason concerns its cute lettering, so P1 is evidence about typography rather than an endorsement of every ingredient in that composition. The previous Shared worlds feedback separately favors shared discovery as a narrative premise.
- **Lead with the practical promise.** The selected specimen reads “Your image. Ready to share.” followed by “Bring in an image. Upload it. Copy the Markdown for your conversation.” Keep usefulness explicit while the cats supply personality. This is a copy direction, not approved final wording.
- **Character design is the next priority, with a less babyish result.** Preserve the detailed watercolor and paper craft the user previously liked. Explore less exaggerated head and eye proportions and more varied builds, while retaining warmth and expression. These are proposed adjustments to compare, not an inferred diagnosis of exactly what felt babyish. The earlier interest in a baby-kitten cast member can coexist with a less infantile overall cast.
- **Reading ease limits novelty.** There is no specimen literally titled “by the way.” The likely reference is “Back to your day,” whose display font is Basteleur. Treat Basteleur as a readability concern and avoid making it a leading candidate; the identification remains provisional. Font names are now visible on each postcard to make future feedback precise.

The [Shared worlds feedback](shared-discovery.md) still governs continuity, anatomy, all-black coats, varied builds, and curious peers. The [storyboard](../storyboard.md) combines the two sets of feedback. No artwork was redrawn in this pass.
