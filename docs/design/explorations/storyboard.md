## What the story is trying to do

The [approved website](../../../web/README.md) now implements the requested pinned, crossfading sequence. Its [notes](website-notes.md) carry the latest direction: preserve the integrated `v3` artwork, keep copy outside it, and use one heading and one short explanation per scene. The independent flower overlay was rejected for incorrect contact and depth; its construction details below are historical.

A person already has an image and something to say. The unfinished part is getting that image into the conversation. Gatto helps finish that small job. The emotional promise we can explore is relief, with a little affection attached to it.

The latest [Shared worlds feedback](feedback/shared-discovery.md) gives us a more specific candidate story: **curious cats discover together how to get an image ready for a conversation.** The main cat is learning too. There is no need for a mentor, and the visitor can connect with whichever character they prefer. The story should make sense before anyone reads an explanation of its symbolism.

This is not a story about how complicated the app is, a rescue from a manufactured catastrophe, or a grand new way of living. Its scale is deliberately small. A small useful thing can have a generous visual world without making an exaggerated promise.

The practical spine stays literal: what the app does, what the result looks like, what it takes to use it, and where to get it. The metaphor supplies atmosphere and memory. It must never change the apparent product behavior.

## Three layers, kept distinct

**Collection:** the mood board is a deliberately unresolved set of things we admire. Anime stills, watery edges, pen studies, unusual letters, precise controls, and several palettes can coexist there. Their disagreement is material for a decision. The board is not a screen-by-screen proposal.

**Grammar:** the system study chooses one provisional combination, Pond paper, so we can examine relationships. It has a heading voice, a quiet reading voice, color roles, contours, a spacing rhythm, and a small motion vocabulary. It is a specimen catalog, not the marketing site.

**Sequence:** this document asks how a visitor could connect a character, an action, and a useful result. The beats below are narrative studies; they do not prescribe page height, navigation, or exact composition.

## Why these references are in the room

The recent [TanStack redesign announcement](https://tanstack.com/blog/tanstack-has-a-new-look) connects the identity to an underlying feeling and describes turning scattered taste into shared design decisions. Its [catalog](https://tanstack.com/ds/) separates foundations and component examples. That is the useful precedent: make the reasons and rules visible, then check the pieces against them. We are not adopting its typography, palette, mascot, or component silhouettes.

The other website pins offer different lessons. Teenage Engineering is a counterweight to excess softness: an interesting object can sit inside a quiet, precise frame. Poolsuite demonstrates commitment to a coherent little world. The Shantell specimen turns lettering variation into something approachable and playful. These are our readings of the references; actual observed values are labeled separately in the research notes.

The art references ask narrower questions. Character relationships can be communicated by pose and distance; the earlier dog reference has been set aside. The current direction uses cats only. A flight still suggests breathing room around a small figure. The watercolor-like and ink-like scenes suggest that atmosphere need not require dense detail. Ribot's studies suggest how a dark mark and a few curves can carry recognition. None establishes that a particular art style will improve downloads.

Borrow the lesson, then redraw the problem with our own character, gesture, setting, and composition. “Like that exact film character” is not an adequate original brief. “A small dark silhouette against quiet paper, with an alert posture” is a transferable visual principle.

## The cats and the place

### The cat is the anchor

Keep the black cat recognizable through pointed ears, a readable silhouette, expressive posture, and a sense of capability. The existing rocket-cat illustration is a continuity reference. We can investigate a brush, watercolor, cut-paper, or smooth cel-shaded treatment without claiming that its identity is already being replaced.

Character acting should be economical. A glance toward the image, a small preparation, a purposeful movement, a settled pose. Avoid making the cat repeatedly fail so the page has something to animate. The visitor is considering a useful tool; the character can be funny without making the tool seem unreliable.

### A varied cast of black cats

The user has ruled out dogs and prefers all the cats to remain black. Watercolor-and-ink is the first favorite, layered paper the second, and selected luminous treatments remain useful for continuity with the app. Explore stronger differences in build: a long, slender cat, a round cat, and a small baby kitten. Eye colors, ears, muzzles, tails, posture, and material can add individuality. One candidate could have differently colored eyes and saturated, almost glowing accents. The material-to-character assignments and exact designs remain open.

The current [shared-world study](multiverse-study.html) explores cats attending to one picture, while the [postcards](identity-postcards.html) use composed encounters and distinct actions. Shared staging and action should connect the materials without making their surfaces identical. The user found continuity and anatomy problems in these images; they are unresolved studies, not examples of a settled character system. See the [mixed-media research](../../research/mixed-media-character-worlds.md).

### Continuity belongs to each individual

A long cat and a round cat can coexist. Consistency means the long cat stays recognizably that individual in the next picture: its relative height, build, eye colors, ears, and other chosen identifying traits do not drift accidentally. A new pose or a change in perspective should explain any apparent difference. We have not decided which traits will define each individual; the original U4 question was unclear and remains unanswered.

Anatomy is a separate drawing check. Trace each visible paw back through its leg to the body. Check joints, weight, overlaps, and the grip on a shared object before polishing texture. Keep physical interaction, which the user likes, but make it understandable. Distinct poses should express different kinds of curiosity without assigning every action to a new, unrecognizable cat.

### The place supports the action

A resting surface, a pale opening, a warm rectangle of light, perhaps a distant line. Start with that much. The scene should offer a place to begin and a place to arrive. A large city, elaborate room, or navigable game world would add decisions before we know whether it helps.

The black cat must remain dark in both website appearances. In a dark scene, put a lighter area behind its silhouette or provide a restrained edge light. Do not reverse the cat to white just because text changes color.

## Why Pond paper is the first system experiment

This is a working synthesis, not the winner of a test. The mood board keeps Apricot dusk and Blue hour available as alternatives.

| Deep green-blue ink        | Capability and definition      | Cat, lettering, and important structure have weight       | Losing the black cat against dark surroundings     |

The proposal uses color families, not a rigid limit on the number of swatches. Functional success and error colors are separate from illustration accents. The system study computes contrast for specific pairs rather than assuming a nice palette is automatically usable.

Coconat now leads the display-font exploration: the user specifically liked its cute lettering on the Shared discovery postcard. Apfel remains the body-font candidate. Ortica offers a more storybook alternative. Basteleur is a likely readability concern: the user's “by the way” reference probably means the Back to your day postcard, but that identification remains provisional. Keep the specimen for reference rather than promoting it as a leading candidate. Shantell is an annotation audition on the board. All are independently distributed under the SIL Open Font License; popularity has not been measured.

The [postcard feedback](feedback/identity-postcards.md) also favors the practical promise: “Your image. Ready to share,” supported by the explicit bring-in, upload, and copy sequence. The same feedback calls the current effect too babyish and prioritizes character design. Keep the detailed materials the user liked while comparing more restrained head and eye proportions and the already-requested varied builds. The exact source of the babyish feeling is still unresolved; do not discard the favorite artwork or the possible baby-kitten member of the cast on that assumption.

## Movement is part of the meaning

The proposed rhythm is anticipation, one purposeful movement, then rest. This can make the cat feel capable and the result feel finished. It is a creative hypothesis, not proof of a conversion benefit.

The study's short timing values help compare sensations. They are not universal perceptual laws. No motion runs on page load in these artifacts. Demonstrations can be restarted deliberately. Reduced motion changes the scene directly to its understandable result; it does not remove the information.

Avoid a restless companion: perpetual bobbing, constantly blinking lights, confetti after trivial clicks, or a cat that follows the pointer while someone tries to read. A character is allowed to be still. The page can feel alive because it responds well when someone chooses to act.

## What to learn before drawing the website

The redraw has now been made. The user likes all three current encounters and wants them retained as one developing scene: the cats find the image, put it in the GitHub box, then look at it there. The [scrolling sequence](story-sequence.html) studies practical text alongside those exact three images. The [redraw notes](art/redraw-notes.md) record the implementation and limits. Preserve the artwork while refining this writing; the small GitHub label belongs to the upload moment. Earlier anatomy concerns below describe why this redraw was needed, not a request to discard the current scenes.

Use the board's temporary Keep controls to make a short list. Type the same ordinary sentence into several font specimens. Look at the palette alternatives beside the black cat rather than judging isolated swatches.

Then compare the system in light and dark, with Coconat and Ortica, and with motion disabled. Check whether the pieces remain related after the novel illustration is removed. If the entire identity disappears, the art is carrying too much of the system alone.

The next experiment should resolve two narrow questions before adding more elaborate scenes:

1. **Can we recognize each cat twice, with a less babyish overall cast?** Make a small cast lineup with long, round, and baby-kitten builds, then two simple encounter panels with different actions. Compare more restrained proportions without losing the favored watercolor and paper detail. Keep each cat’s own material, eyes, proportions, and relative scale consistent. Check silhouettes and limb connections before adding detailed textures. This is a new study brief; the current art has not been repaired.
2. **Can the story explain one real workflow?** Sketch only “bring an image → upload → copy the result,” using the same identifiable picture throughout. Put the literal action next to each emotional beat. Use an illustrated image and a labeled result rather than screenshots. Ask whether a visitor can explain what Gatto does after viewing it.

These are separate checks: beautiful anatomy cannot fix an unclear product story, and a clear caption cannot repair a misplaced paw. Do not generate a whole site, a complete fictional world, or a final cast to answer them. The expanded exploration’s existing sheets and encounters remain available in the [artwork notes](art/README.md).

Before choosing a direction, resolve only the decisions that materially change the system: heading voice, art material, warm/cool balance, and how a secondary cat or alternate self adds something useful. Keep the information path clear while those decisions are open. There is no need to choose a page template, stack, or final screen composition to answer them.

This pass changes no app behavior and publishes nothing. It produces a collection, a candidate grammar, and a narrative to react to.
